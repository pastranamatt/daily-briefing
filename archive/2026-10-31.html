Apple’s first touchscreen MacBook Pro arrives at premium prices

Apple unveiled redesigned 14- and 16-inch touchscreen MacBook Pros with OLED displays, M5 Pro or Max chips and a touch-friendly interface. Apple also refreshes its base MacBook Pro with M6

The separate 14-inch M6 MacBook Pro starts at $1,999, with Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and a claimed 24-hour battery life. Apple opened preorders October 28 and targets November 6 availability. Its stated graphics and AI speed gains are manufacturer benchmarks, not independent tests.

03Oct 29 · TechCrunch

Rivian’s R2 is already its best-selling EV

Rivian said the lower-priced R2 SUV led its vehicle sales in the third quarter, just months after launch. It raised the floor of its 2026 delivery outlook to 67,000 vehicles; Q3 deliveries totaled 19,248. The company is still loss-making, but the R2 ramp is an important test of mass-market demand.

Intel’s guidance shock triggers a semiconductor selloff

Intel shares fell roughly 30% after its revenue outlook missed expectations by more than $1 billion. Weak PC demand, memory prices and costly manufacturing expansion raised questions about the company’s turnaround and rattled the broader chip sector.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio urged cooperation on security, energy and supply chains, but Russia’s presence and disputes over Ukraine and the Middle East made a consensus declaration unlikely. Several delegations objected to a group photograph with Russia’s foreign minister.

Qatar renews mediation between Washington and Tehran

Qatari mediators returned to Tehran seeking a framework for a US–Iran agreement. Talks remain divided over sequencing: Washington wants nuclear issues addressed immediately, while Iran seeks an end to the US blockade and relief for oil exports first. No settlement has been announced.

Israel’s election produces no clear governing majority

The final count gives Netanyahu’s bloc 53 of 120 seats, the Eisenkot-led opposition 48 and Arab parties 19. Both major blocs lack the 61 seats needed to govern on their own, leaving coalition negotiations — and the caretaker government’s future — uncertain.

Washington approves a $2.1 billion Patriot sale to Ukraine

The State Department approved a proposed sale of 500 PAC-3 interceptors, funded through partner-country contributions and Ukrainian financing. Approval does not mean rapid delivery: production backlogs remain a critical issue as Russia continues strikes on Ukrainian cities and infrastructure.

Space News

01Oct 30–31 · Spaceflight Now

SpaceX reaches Falcon 9 launch No. 700

A Starlink mission lifted off from Vandenberg at 9:04 p.m. Pacific on October 30 (12:04 a.m. Eastern October 31), carrying 27 satellites. The first-stage booster landed successfully. The flight marked the 700th Falcon 9 launch, a milestone as SpaceX shifts development attention toward Starship.

Read original report ↗
02Oct 29 · Space.com

SpaceX’s Starfall capsule tests rapid military cargo delivery

The Air Force Research Laboratory recovered test cargo after the Starfall return capsule flew on SpaceX’s October 20 Bandwagon-5 mission. The experiment gathered reentry and landing data for possible point-to-point military logistics; it was a demonstration, not an operational delivery service.

Read original report ↗
03Oct 30 · Economic Times

India launches a new satellite for its NavIC navigation network

India’s GSLV-F18 placed the NVS-03 satellite into its planned transfer orbit. The spacecraft includes an indigenous atomic clock and is intended to strengthen India’s regional satellite-navigation capability.

04Oct 28 · Reuters

Perseverance finds exceptionally fine Martian dust

NASA’s rover measured dust particles as small as roughly 100 nanometers. Understanding how this material behaves on suits and inside habitats will help engineers plan cleaning systems and protect future Mars crews.

A Reuters analysis traces the Pentagon’s rapid deployment of Maven and commercial AI models in military intelligence workflows.
