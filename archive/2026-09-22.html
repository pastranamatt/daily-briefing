1. Artificial Intelligence

Alibaba unveils a much larger AI model plan and a new in-house chip

Alibaba announced plans for a next-generation model in the 5–10 trillion parameter range and introduced its Zhenwu V900 accelerator, which it says delivers roughly three times the performance of its predecessor. The full-stack push — models, chips and data centers — is another sign that China's largest technology companies are trying to reduce dependence on U.S. AI infrastructure.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026

Meta's Muse is starting to look like a new revenue engine

Wall Street is warming rapidly to Meta's agentic assistant Muse, which can handle tasks such as email, travel booking and transactions. Reuters reports 2.8 million downloads in its first 12 days and a more than 20% rise in Meta shares since launch, showing how quickly investor attention is shifting from model capability toward monetizable agents.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026

Banks warn agentic shopping could outrun consumer protections

A group including Bank of America, Capital One, ING and NatWest is warning that AI shopping agents create new fraud, privacy and payment risks. Their proposals include disclosure when AI participates in a transaction, stronger data safeguards and interoperability — an early preview of the rules financial institutions may demand before agentic commerce scales.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026

OpenAI and Anthropic push Australia to loosen AI-training restrictions

Both labs are asking Canberra for a limited copyright exemption that would allow model training on Australian creative material under defined conditions. The dispute captures the core policy tradeoff now appearing globally: attracting AI infrastructure investment while protecting creators whose work supplies training data.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026

2. Foreign Affairs

Iran signals an opening on Hormuz as leaders gather at the UN

Tehran says it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz to Gulf shipping if Washington lifts its blockade, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio says the U.S. is open to talks with Iran if they can produce a meaningful result. No direct meeting is scheduled, but the UN General Assembly has become a potential diplomatic off-ramp amid high oil prices and regional escalation.

Sources: Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026; Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026

Russia and Ukraine trade deadly strikes ahead of Zelenskyy's Trump meeting

Russian attacks killed five people across Ukraine while Ukraine struck Russia's Kuibyshev oil refinery. Zelenskyy is in New York seeking more air-defense support and preparing to meet President Trump as attacks on energy infrastructure again become central to the war.

Source: Associated Press — Sept. 22, 2026

G7 tells Iran to stop arming the Houthis

G7 foreign ministers meeting around the UN General Assembly called on Iran to end military support for the Houthis and urged the group to stop attacks on shipping and regional targets. The statement underscores how Red Sea security and the Iran confrontation are increasingly being treated as one connected regional problem.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026

3. Space News

Today is Starship's first orbital attempt

SpaceX is targeting Flight 14 from Starbase this morning, with the mission designed to put Starship into stable Earth orbit for the first time. The nearly 10-hour flight would complete about six orbits and deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites before a Pacific splashdown. If successful, it would be the program's most consequential milestone yet.

Sources: Reuters — Sept. 15, 2026; Space.com — Sept. 16, 2026

Flight 14 doubles as the debut of operational Starlink V3 deployment

The 26 V3 spacecraft make today's mission more than a vehicle test. SpaceX is attempting to demonstrate that Starship can begin doing useful orbital work while it is still in development, tying the rocket's economics directly to the next generation of Starlink.

Source: Ars Technica — Sept. 15, 2026

HEO raises $25 million for satellite-to-satellite imagery

Australian startup HEO raised a Series B to expand its unusual “flyby” imaging service, which uses sensors aboard other spacecraft to photograph satellites in orbit. The company plans to extend the capability into geostationary orbit, where improved inspection and awareness have growing commercial and national-security value.

Source: Axios — Sept. 21, 2026

Crusoe's $3.9 billion round puts AI infrastructure back at megadeal scale

Crusoe raised a Series F at a $30.9 billion valuation, co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with Founders Fund, Nvidia, QIA and others participating. The size of the deal shows how much private capital is still flowing toward power, data centers and compute rather than only model companies.

Source: TechCrunch — Sept. 17, 2026

Ramona Optics raises $25 million from ARCH and other deep-tech investors

The Durham microscopy startup raised a Series A led by ARCH Venture Partners with Stealthpoint Ventures and ND Capital. Its systems aim to analyze biological samples at greater scale and speed, a reminder that venture dollars are also flowing into enabling scientific hardware beyond the generative-AI boom.

Source: Axios — Sept. 22, 2026

Morphotonics raises €40 million and pivots nanoimprint technology toward data centers

The Dutch company, backed by 3M Ventures, Innovation Industries, BOM and Invest-NL, is scaling nanoimprint lithography used in optics and expanding into optical components for data centers — another example of AI infrastructure demand pulling adjacent manufacturing technologies into the compute stack.

Source: TechCrunch — Sept. 21, 2026

5. Defense Industry

Dassault flight-tests AI algorithms aboard a Rafale fighter

Dassault Aviation says it successfully tested two AI algorithms in flight, including one developed with Thales. The company says the systems are mature enough to be considered for future Rafale upgrades and are intended to assist human crews — part of the broader shift toward AI-enabled combat aviation and autonomous wingmen.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22, 2026

General Atomics delivers FQ-42 Vengeance to Creech for expanded CCA testing

A new FQ-42 Vengeance has arrived at Creech Air Force Base for continued test and evaluation. The delivery comes as the Air Force experiments with both General Atomics' aircraft and Anduril's FQ-44 Fury, moving Collaborative Combat Aircraft from prototype programs toward operational tactics and force integration.

Source: Defense News — Sept. 21, 2026

Anduril says the delayed $14 billion Taiwan arms package is affecting business

Palmer Luckey says stalled U.S. approval of the Taiwan package is slowing progress beyond the specific systems covered by the sale. Anduril already supplies Altius drones and has co-developed the Barracuda-500 with Taiwanese partners, putting it directly at the intersection of U.S.-China diplomacy and Taiwan's asymmetric-defense buildup.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 19, 2026

Luckey wants Taiwan to turn commercial manufacturing strength into a defense export base

In Taipei, Luckey argued that Taiwan is moving faster than most nations on asymmetric systems and should design weapons around factories, workers and supply chains that already exist. He urged Taiwan to build not only for domestic deterrence but for export, using strengths in semiconductors, precision machinery, optics and composites to make defense production economically sustainable.

Source: Taiwan News / CNA — Sept. 20, 2026
