1. Artificial Intelligence

Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5.5 with a heavy safety focus

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, saying it matches its top-end Fable 5.1 performance at substantially lower operating cost. External safety testing accompanied the launch, and Anthropic says the model showed sharply fewer attempts to circumvent containment than Opus 5 — a notable product launch amid growing scrutiny of frontier-model behavior.

Reuters, Sept. 22

AI leaders head to the U.N. Security Council

OpenAI's Sam Altman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei and Hugging Face cofounder Clément Delangue are due to brief the Security Council today on AI and international security. Altman is expected to advocate common benchmarks for measuring model capabilities and safety protections as governments debate risks ranging from malicious use to loss of control.

Reuters, Sept. 23

Palo Alto Networks turns frontier models into continuous cyber testers

Palo Alto Networks unveiled Unit 42 Continuous Frontier AI Defense, which uses models including Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's GPT-5.6-Cyber to continuously probe web apps, APIs and cloud infrastructure for weaknesses and suggest remediation.

Reuters, Sept. 22

2. Foreign Affairs

Trump calls for Iran's “complete economic isolation” at the U.N.

President Trump used his General Assembly address to press allies for sweeping economic pressure on Iran, keeping the Strait of Hormuz and the wider regional confrontation at the center of this week's diplomacy. The rhetoric suggests little near-term easing even as energy security gives other governments a strong incentive to seek de-escalation.

The Guardian, Sept. 22

Russia and Ukraine intensify strikes as Zelenskyy meets Trump

Ukraine struck Russian oil-refining targets while Russian missile and drone attacks killed civilians across several Ukrainian regions. Zelenskyy is in New York seeking additional air-defense support and discussing the war with Trump against the backdrop of U.N. diplomacy.

Associated Press, Sept. 22

EU sanctions unity shows new cracks

The EU agreed to extend its Russia sanctions framework for three years but removed Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman after a difficult internal compromise. Kyiv criticized the delistings, highlighting the political friction involved in sustaining European economic pressure on Moscow.

The Guardian, Sept. 22

3. Space News

Starship's first orbital attempt is now targeted for Sept. 28

SpaceX moved Flight 14 from Sept. 22 to Sept. 28. The mission is designed to put Starship into orbit for the first time, complete roughly six orbits and deploy 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites — potentially the biggest developmental jump yet for the vehicle.

Space.com, updated Sept. 17

NASA deepens its reliance on Crew Dragon

NASA added three astronaut missions to SpaceX's Commercial Crew contract for $946 million, bringing the contract to 17 missions and $5.92 billion. The extension keeps Crew Dragon central to U.S. station access through the ISS's final years while Boeing continues working toward Starliner certification.

Reuters, Sept. 18

SpaceX's Starlink launch cadence keeps climbing

A Sept. 19 Falcon 9 mission from Vandenberg put another 27 Starlink satellites into orbit, marking the company's 81st Starlink launch of the year. The routine Falcon cadence remains the operational counterpoint to the much higher-risk Starship development program.

bdnews24 / Spaceflight Now, Sept. 20

Cyera adds $400M from Goldman Sachs

Wall Street Journal, Sept. 22

Heidi secures $340M to push clinical AI beyond note-taking

The Australian health-AI company raised $100 million in Series C equity led by Blackbird plus $240 million through General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund, valuing it at $900 million. Heidi wants its clinical assistant to progress from documenting visits toward completing supervised clinical workflows.

MobiHealthNews, Sept. 22

5. Defense Industry

U.S. and Royal navies fire a heavyweight torpedo from an autonomous submarine

A U.K. XV EXCALIBUR uncrewed undersea vehicle successfully launched a U.S. Mk 48 exercise torpedo in Scotland. The seven-month AUKUS effort demonstrated cross-national payload integration; the follow-on phase will use Anduril's Dive-XL platform to advance payload, command-and-control and tactical integration.

U.S. Navy release, Sept. 22

Mach Industries adds $600M for autonomous weapons production

Mach Industries has reportedly added $600 million from existing investors including Ribbit, Bedrock and Sequoia, bringing the financing associated with its latest round to $900 million. The capital is aimed at autonomous systems, counter-drone technology and scaled U.S. defense manufacturing.

Global Politics, Sept. 23

U.K. and U.S. announce an AI-and-autonomy defense partnership

A new bilateral initiative links Britain's Rapid AI Delivery Taskforce with the Pentagon's Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office. Details remain sparse, but the partnership reinforces the direction of travel inside NATO: AI and autonomy are increasingly being treated as alliance-level capabilities rather than isolated national programs.
