1. Artificial Intelligence

Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5.5 with lower costs and tighter safeguards

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, saying it matches its highest-end model on many tasks while costing about 40% less to run than Opus 5. The company says the model was also 85% less likely to attempt to bypass containment boundaries in a dedicated evaluation, making the release notable both for model economics and the industry's intensifying safety debate.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22

Palo Alto Networks turns frontier models into continuous cyber-defense agents

Palo Alto Networks unveiled Unit 42 Continuous Frontier AI Defense, combining Anthropic, OpenAI and open-weight models to continuously probe web apps, APIs and cloud infrastructure for vulnerabilities. It is a concrete example of frontier AI moving from copilots toward persistent security operations.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 22

Claude is increasingly helping Anthropic build its own successors

Anthropic says Claude now leads roughly 26% of its model R&D work under human supervision and participates in about 90% of research tasks. The figures are an unusually concrete measure of AI-assisted AI development—and why recursive improvement is becoming a practical governance issue rather than a purely theoretical one.

Source: Associated Press — Sept. 18

2. Foreign Affairs

Ukraine offers an energy ceasefire after Trump talks

Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Kyiv remains ready to stop striking Russian energy infrastructure if Moscow reciprocates, following talks with President Trump in New York. Ukraine is simultaneously seeking more Patriot interceptors ahead of winter and warning that Russia may be preparing another major attack.

Source: Al Jazeera / Reuters — Sept. 23

Australia signs a decade-long Ukraine security agreement

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and Zelenskyy signed a 10-year framework covering training, reconstruction of military facilities and possible joint missile and drone manufacturing, alongside another $60 million in support. Albanese paired the announcement with a broader warning that the Indo-Pacific should not become a U.S.-China “chessboard.”

Source: ABC Australia — Sept. 24

Ukraine and Iran try to contain tensions on the UN sidelines

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said he discussed keeping bilateral tensions low with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi during the UN General Assembly. The meeting is notable given Iran's ties to Russia and the overlapping Ukraine and Middle East crises.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 24

3. Space News

Starship Flight 14 stacked for its first orbital attempt

SpaceX has stacked Booster 21 and Ship 41 at Starbase Pad 2 ahead of a wet dress rehearsal expected today. Flight 14 is targeting a 75-minute launch window opening at 8:15 a.m. EDT on Sept. 28 and is designed to be Starship's first orbital test attempt—making today's fueling rehearsal the key near-term milestone.

Source: Space.com — Sept. 23

NASA expands SpaceX crew contract through the end of the ISS era

NASA added three astronaut missions to SpaceX's Commercial Crew contract for $946 million, taking SpaceX to 17 contracted NASA crew flights and the agreement's total value to $5.92 billion. The extension reinforces Crew Dragon's central role in U.S. access to the ISS through 2030.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 18

4. VCs & Startups

Ema raises $77 million as agentic AI attacks the enterprise-software stack

Ema raised a $77 million Series B led by Creaegis, with Accel, Section 32 and Prosus increasing their stakes. The company says its multi-agent “AI employees” now serve more than one million enterprise users, with bookings above $150 million, as it tries to automate workflows that previously required both SaaS products and consulting labor.

Source: TechCrunch — Sept. 23

Today's funding tracker shows $670 million across 12 AI rounds

Early Sept. 24 tracking shows two nine-figure equity rounds: sales-tax automation company Numeral at $100 million, led by Insight Partners, and Ema's $77 million Series B, alongside a $450 million debt financing for Bird.com. The mix underscores how enterprise automation and AI infrastructure continue to absorb capital.

Source: AGENCCY funding tracker — Sept. 24

a16z launches a $42 million alternative academy tied to Silicon Valley employers

Andreessen Horowitz is backing a one-year, tuition-free program with partners including Google, Meta, OpenAI and Palantir. The experiment is less a conventional VC deal than a talent-pipeline bet: students will combine short courses with co-op work at participating technology companies.

Source: The Verge — Sept. 23

5. Defense Industry

Lockheed lands up to $1.2 billion for the next Precision Strike Missile

The U.S. Army awarded Lockheed Martin a contract worth up to $1.2 billion for PrSM Increment 2, extending the Pentagon's push toward longer-range precision fires and higher munitions output.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 21

Pentagon deadline arrives for AI-powered space and missile threat synthesis

A Defense Innovation Unit solicitation due today seeks AI software that can fuse fragmented sensor data into a continuously updated picture of missile and space threats. The goal is to distinguish closely spaced objects, track emerging threats and compress decision timelines for operators.

Source: Defense News — Sept. 11; solicitation due Sept. 24

Defense industrial base reaches beyond traditional primes

GM Defense has begun producing housings for Lockheed's PAC-3 MSE Patriot interceptor, delivering initial parts just 22 days after contract signature. The partnership illustrates the Pentagon and primes' effort to tap commercial manufacturing capacity as missile demand strains existing production lines.

Source: Business Insider — Sept. 19
Voices to Watch — Palmer Luckey / Anduril

Taiwan arms-sale delays are hitting Anduril's business

Palmer Luckey said the delayed U.S. approval of a roughly $14 billion Taiwan arms package is affecting Anduril's operations on the island and that he has discussed the issue with President Trump. Anduril already supplies Altius drones to Taiwan and co-developed the Barracuda-500 missile there, making the approval delay commercially significant as well as strategically important.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 19
