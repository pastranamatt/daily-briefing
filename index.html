1. Artificial Intelligence

FTC opens a broad probe into OpenAI, Anthropic and AI-agent safety

The Federal Trade Commission has opened an investigation into leading AI labs, including OpenAI and Anthropic, focused on consumer risks from increasingly autonomous agents. The probe follows recent incidents in which AI systems reportedly interacted with external services in ways that raised concerns about cybersecurity, permissions and accountability.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 30, 2026

White House AI accord formalizes voluntary self-policing commitments

Major AI and technology companies signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, a voluntary framework centered on internal controls, outside audits and board-level review. The agreement is not legally binding, but it is the clearest signal yet of the administration's preference for industry-led safeguards over sweeping federal regulation.

Sources: Associated Press — Sept. 30, 2026; Axios — Sept. 30, 2026

HPE lands a $1.2 billion AI infrastructure order from Vultr

Hewlett Packard Enterprise disclosed a $1.2 billion order from cloud provider Vultr for AMD-powered server racks supporting AI workloads. The deal reinforces how quickly capital spending is shifting toward networking, compute and data-center infrastructure rather than only model developers.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 30, 2026

2. Foreign Affairs

Russia launches one of its heaviest recent attacks on Ukraine's power grid

Russia launched nearly 190 drones and ballistic missiles in a coordinated assault on Ukrainian energy infrastructure, killing at least seven people and damaging facilities across multiple regions. The scale of the strike raises fears of another deliberate winter campaign against heating and electricity systems.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 30, 2026

Mediators push to break the U.S.–Iran deadlock over Hormuz

Diplomats are circulating an amended interim proposal aimed at easing tensions around the Strait of Hormuz. The core dispute remains sequencing: Iran wants sanctions relief and access to frozen assets, while Washington is pressing for broader concessions on the nuclear program and regional security.

Source: Financial Times — Sept. 30, 2026

U.S. Senate pressure grows over delayed Taiwan security assistance

Senate Armed Services Committee chair Roger Wicker said U.S. security commitments to Taiwan must be upheld and joined other senators pressing the administration to release more than $15 billion in stalled assistance. The dispute follows renewed debate over arms transfers after the latest U.S.–China summit.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 30, 2026

3. Space News

Starship's first orbital mission is now the benchmark for SpaceX's next phase

Starship Flight 14 reached orbit and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, a milestone that changes the program's near-term focus from simply achieving orbit to improving reliability, mission duration and reusability. An early engine issue shortened the flight, but the primary orbital and payload-deployment objectives were completed.

Sources: Reuters — Sept. 28, 2026; Associated Press — Sept. 28, 2026

SpaceX prepares NASA Crew-13 for an October 1 launch

NASA's Crew-13 mission is scheduled to launch aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 and Dragon no earlier than 11:10 a.m. EDT Thursday. The Space Force currently gives the mission a 60% chance of favorable weather, with cumulus clouds and precipitation the main concerns.

Source: NASA — Sept. 29, 2026

SpaceX shifts Starship talent into its growing supercompute operation

SpaceX has moved veteran launch-operations executive Kevin Holland into a senior role overseeing its Memphis supercompute buildout, reflecting the growing overlap between SpaceX and xAI infrastructure following their merger earlier this year.

Source: Business Insider — Sept. 30, 2026

5. Defense Industry

Pentagon awards Boeing a $20 billion F/A-XX development contract

Boeing has won the Navy's contract to develop the next-generation F/A-XX stealth fighter, a program intended to replace the F/A-18 Super Hornet and EA-18G Growler in the 2030s. The award gives Boeing major positions in both the Air Force's and Navy's sixth-generation fighter programs.

Source: Financial Times — Sept. 30, 2026

Raytheon's $20.7 billion AMRAAM deal underscores stockpile pressure

The Pentagon awarded RTX's Raytheon a provisional multiyear contract worth up to $20.7 billion to accelerate AMRAAM production. The agreement reflects growing concern over missile inventories after sustained demand from Ukraine and Middle East operations.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 29, 2026

Taiwan arms backlog becomes a more visible Washington issue

Lawmakers are increasing pressure on the administration to move already-approved security assistance to Taiwan, with concerns that procurement delays could weaken deterrence as Chinese military pressure increases.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 30, 2026
