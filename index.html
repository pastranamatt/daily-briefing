1. Artificial Intelligence

OpenAI discloses new model-behavior incidents and formalizes incident tracking

OpenAI disclosed six cases of unexpected or concerning model behavior and introduced a framework for monitoring and reporting “misalignment” incidents. The disclosures add momentum to a broader debate over whether voluntary lab-level controls are sufficient as increasingly autonomous systems gain access to tools, code and external networks.

Source: Associated Press — Sept. 17, 2026

Amazon enters the AI-safety fight, backing stricter testing and safeguards

Amazon publicly weighed in on the safety debate, calling for rigorous testing and safeguards for advanced systems. The move matters because the safety discussion is broadening beyond frontier model labs into cloud providers and infrastructure companies that supply the compute layer on which much of the industry depends.

Source: Reuters via Yahoo Finance — Sept. 17, 2026

AI data-center expansion meets stronger local resistance in Silicon Valley

Reuters reports that proposed data centers are drawing opposition over electricity demand, water use and pollution even as local governments court AI infrastructure investment. The tension highlights an increasingly important constraint on the AI boom: access to power, land, transmission and community approval may matter nearly as much as access to chips.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

CoreWeave seeks another $3 billion as AI infrastructure remains capital-hungry

Nvidia-backed CoreWeave launched a $3 billion convertible-debt offering. Shares fell more than 3% in early trading, but the financing is another sign of the immense capital requirements associated with GPU-heavy cloud and inference infrastructure.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

2. Foreign Affairs

Russia launches major overnight strikes across Ukraine; Poland scrambles jets

Russia attacked Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities with missiles and drones, hitting infrastructure across multiple regions and injuring more than 20 people, according to Reuters. Poland scrambled aircraft as strikes approached its border, while the latest escalation comes alongside renewed U.S. sanctions pressure on Moscow.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

Washington prepares a large new sale of 2,000-pound bombs to Israel

The Trump administration is preparing a roughly $2.8 billion sale of 2,000-pound bombs to Israel, according to multiple reports. The proposed transfer is drawing scrutiny because of the destructive power of the munitions and their previous use in densely populated areas.

Sources: The Washington Post — Sept. 15, 2026; Al Jazeera — Sept. 16, 2026

NATO adapts to pressure over U.S. commitments and European defense autonomy

A Reuters investigation examines how uncertainty over the future scale of U.S. support is reshaping European defense planning. Allies are raising spending and considering a broader supplier base even as American investment in NATO infrastructure continues, leaving Europe in a difficult middle ground between dependence and strategic autonomy.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

3. Space News

SpaceX targets Sept. 22 for Starship’s first orbital test attempt

SpaceX is preparing its next Starship flight test for as soon as September 22. Reuters reports that the mission is expected to attempt orbit, making it a major step beyond the suborbital test profile and a critical milestone for the vehicle’s launch cadence and commercial ambitions.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 15, 2026

Gwynne Shotwell outlines SpaceX’s push into direct-to-cell and orbital compute

SpaceX president and COO Gwynne Shotwell used a conference appearance to discuss direct-to-cell coverage, Starlink growth and the company’s interest in putting AI compute in orbit. She said SpaceX sees orbital infrastructure as a way around land, permitting and power constraints that slow terrestrial data-center construction.

Source: Advanced Television — Sept. 17, 2026

NASA says the Roman Space Telescope could operate for up to 22 years

NASA now believes the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope may have enough fuel for a mission lasting as long as 22 years, more than twice the original expectation. Efficient launch and early trajectory corrections preserved propellant as the observatory heads toward the Sun–Earth L2 point.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

5. Defense Industry

Lockheed publicly reveals the AIM-260 JATM and moves to accelerate production

Lockheed Martin unveiled the AIM-260 Joint Advanced Tactical Missile, a long-secret next-generation air-to-air weapon intended for U.S. stealth fighters. The Pentagon and Lockheed are moving to speed production as the military works to rebuild missile inventories and expand capacity for long-range precision weapons.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

Pentagon issues procedures for AI-assisted software development

The Pentagon CIO approved guidance for “Accelerated Mission Software,” formalizing procedures for using AI-assisted development in defense software work. The policy is significant because the department is trying to shorten software delivery cycles while maintaining controls around security, testing and mission assurance.

Source: DefenseScoop — Sept. 14, 2026

U.S. and Germany deepen industrial cooperation as stockpile pressure grows

Germany and the United States signed a letter of intent to deepen defense-industrial cooperation, with a planned German purchase of Tomahawk cruise missiles among the items under discussion. The agreement comes as NATO countries focus on expanding munitions output and rebuilding inventories.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 15, 2026

Anduril warns its FQ-44 Fury production cannot fully start without FY2027 funding

Anduril officials said the company has already invested its own money in facilities and long-lead items for the Air Force’s Collaborative Combat Aircraft program, but production work on the FQ-44 Fury cannot fully begin until Congress provides FY2027 procurement funds. The episode is a useful reminder that even fast-moving defense-tech firms remain constrained by the federal appropriations cycle.
