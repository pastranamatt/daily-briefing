1. Artificial Intelligence

Anthropic says Claude is increasingly helping build its own successor

Anthropic disclosed that Claude now performs a meaningful share of the company’s model-research work under human supervision, including contributions to development of future systems. The company framed the disclosure as part of a push for greater transparency around recursive improvement and increasingly autonomous AI research workflows.

Source: Associated Press — Sept. 18, 2026

European AI firms push back on U.S. calls to slow frontier development

Mistral and other European technology voices are criticizing proposals to slow advanced AI development, arguing that a moratorium could entrench large U.S. incumbents while Europe is still trying to close the capability gap. The dispute is turning AI safety into an industrial-policy and competition issue as well as a technical one.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 18, 2026

AI-assisted bug hunters expose an OpenAI account and repository access flaw

Security researchers participating in OpenAI’s bug bounty used Anthropic’s Claude to help exploit a chain of vulnerabilities that briefly gave them access to an employee account and internal development resources. The researchers reported the issue rather than accessing sensitive data, and OpenAI subsequently fixed the weaknesses.

Source: The Wall Street Journal — Sept. 18, 2026

2. Foreign Affairs

Iran says it struck a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran said its forces struck a Togo-flagged oil tanker that it accused of attempting an unauthorized transit through the Strait of Hormuz. The incident adds to pressure on one of the world’s most important energy chokepoints amid the continuing U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran and broader shipping disruptions in the region.

Source: Associated Press — Sept. 18, 2026

Russia summons the UK envoy over British military support for Ukraine

Moscow summoned Britain’s chargé d’affaires to protest continued weapons support for Ukraine. The move comes as relations between Russia and the UK remain deeply strained and as European states debate additional military aid and sanctions.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 18, 2026

Zelenskyy hosts a new regional summit spanning Ukraine’s neighbors

Leaders from seven countries joined Ukraine for the inaugural “Carpathian Eight” summit, focused on regional security, energy, trade, logistics and cross-border cooperation. The grouping includes states with sharply different positions on military aid to Kyiv, making the forum notable as an attempt to build practical cooperation despite political divisions.

Source: Associated Press — Sept. 18, 2026

3. Space News

NASA selects SpaceX to launch the StarBurst astrophysics mission

NASA selected SpaceX to launch StarBurst, a small satellite mission designed to detect gamma-ray bursts from neutron-star mergers. The mission will support multi-messenger astronomy by helping scientists identify events that can also produce gravitational waves.

Source: Benzinga — Sept. 18, 2026

Starship Flight 14 remains the next major SpaceX test to watch

SpaceX is preparing Starship Flight 14 for a more ambitious test profile as the company continues working toward a rapidly reusable heavy-lift system. Reporting this week indicates the previously discussed Sept. 18 target slipped, so the exact launch timing remains fluid.

Sources: India Today — Sept. 18, 2026; Basenor update — Sept. 15, 2026

Impulse Space’s Series D expands to $808 million

Impulse Space, founded by former SpaceX propulsion chief Tom Mueller, added $308 million to its Series D, bringing the round to $808 million and the company’s valuation to $5.4 billion. The company is scaling manufacturing as its backlog of commercial and government orbital-transfer missions grows.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 16, 2026

4. VCs & Startups

Crusoe raises $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation

AI-infrastructure provider Crusoe raised $3.9 billion in a new round that values the company at $30.9 billion post-money. The financing underscores how much venture and growth capital is still flowing into “neocloud” and data-center businesses positioned around the compute bottleneck.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

Mantic raises $25 million to build AI systems for forecasting

London-based Mantic raised a $25 million seed round led by Radical Ventures, with participation from Microsoft’s M12, Thinking Machines Lab and Balderton Capital. The startup is training AI systems specifically for political, economic and cultural forecasting, a category with obvious applications for markets, government and geopolitical analysis.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 18, 2026

Intel-veteran startup Delos Data raises $100 million for AI networking

Delos Data raised $100 million to build chips and software that improve data movement between processors inside AI data centers. Investors include Matrix Partners, Playground and other venture firms betting that networking and interconnects will become a larger share of the AI-infrastructure stack.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 15, 2026

5. Defense Industry

Lockheed receives the first GM-built parts for Patriot interceptors

Lockheed Martin received its first critical PAC-3 MSE components from GM Defense only weeks after the companies formalized their production agreement. The partnership is part of a broader U.S. effort to accelerate missile output and diversify the defense industrial base as demand remains high from multiple theaters.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

U.S. clears a possible $24.3 billion F-35 sale to Saudi Arabia

The State Department approved a possible sale of 48 F-35 fighters and associated engines to Saudi Arabia, subject to congressional review. If completed, the transaction would be one of the most consequential U.S. arms sales in the Middle East and would raise questions about regional force balance and Israel’s qualitative military edge.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026

Lockheed reveals the AIM-260 and moves to accelerate production

Lockheed publicly unveiled the AIM-260 Joint Advanced Tactical Missile, a next-generation air-to-air weapon intended for U.S. stealth fighters. The Pentagon and Lockheed are now moving to increase production capacity as the U.S. rebuilds missile inventories and prepares for higher-end air combat requirements.

Source: Reuters — Sept. 17, 2026
